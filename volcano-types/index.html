Whakaari / White Island · A volcano can extend far below sea level.

Your task · Read the three research cards. In your own words, describe each type and explain at least two differences. Think about shape, erupted material and how it forms.

Cone / composite

Steep sides and a large volcanic mountain. Repeated eruptions deposit lava and ash near a vent. Relatively viscous lava does not spread as far. Examples: Taranaki and Ruapehu.

Scoria field / shield

A volcanic field contains separate eruption centres. Scoria cones are small hills of thrown-out fragments. Shields have broad, gentle slopes built by runny lava. Auckland contains several forms, including Rangitoto’s shield and scoria cones.

Caldera

A large depression forms when the ground collapses after substantial magma is withdrawn during an eruption. Some fill with water. Lakes Taupō and Rotorua occupy calderas; a caldera is much larger than a typical summit crater.

A useful distinction: “scoria field / shield” is the combined category from the slides. A field is an area containing volcanoes; a scoria cone and a shield are different landforms. In this activity, “cone” means a large composite volcano.
Make the comparison

What makes them different?

Useful words: whereas · steeper · wider · fragments · viscous (resists flowing) · collapse

Check your explanation

Did you link steep composite slopes to material building up near a vent? Did you distinguish a small fragment-built scoria cone from a broad lava shield? Did you explain that a caldera forms through collapse?

For example: “A shield is broader and has gentler slopes than a composite cone because its runnier lava spreads farther. A caldera is a collapse basin, whereas cones and shields are mainly built up by erupted material.”
